L–M–D: worked example

Learn → teach → be taught

The root ל–מ–ד (L–M–D) connects this word family. The underlined letters below belong to the root; the surrounding letters and vowel marks help form the words.

Compare the same form first

These three forms are all third-person masculine singular perfect (3ms qatal). Holding person, gender, number, and conjugation constant makes the pattern relationship easier to see.

Learn · Qal

לָמַד
lamad

“He learned.”

Qal · Perfect (qatal) · Third-person masculine singular.Isaiah 26:10

Teach · Piel

לִמַּד
limmad

“He taught.”

Piel · Perfect (qatal) · Third-person masculine singular.Ecclesiastes 12:9

Be taught · Pual

לֻמַּד
lummad

“He was instructed.”

Pual · Perfect (qatal) · Third-person masculine singular.Jeremiah 31:18

The full pattern, not one vowel alone, distinguishes the forms. “Passive” carries no moral judgment.

See the forms used in the lesson passages

These are real forms from the passages discussed in the lesson. They use different grammatical shapes, so they show the root-and-pattern relationship in context but are not the controlled 3ms comparison above.

Learn · Qal

אֶלְמַ֥ד
elmad

“I may learn.” The speaker is learning.

Qal · Imperfect (yiqtol) · First person common singular.Psalm 119:71

Teach · Piel

מְלַמֵּ֣ד
melammed

“One teaching.” Someone teaches another.

Piel · Active participle · Masculine singular.Psalm 18:34 · Hebrew 18:35

Be taught · Pual

מְלֻמָּדָֽה
melummadah

“Being taught.” In this verse, the word describes a taught precept.

Pual · Passive participle · Feminine singular.Isaiah 29:13

The feminine ending in Isaiah agrees with the noun. These contextual forms reinforce that the root and the whole pattern work together.

In the checked Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible corpus, L–M–D occurs 86 times: 24 Qal, 57 Piel, and 5 Pual. It is attested in three of the seven binyanim, not merely three times. Niphal, Hiphil, Hophal, and Hitpael are not attested for this root in that checked Biblical corpus; this does not make a claim about every later stage of Hebrew.

The learner and the learning

The L–M–D learning family also includes nouns: names for a pupil and for study. These belong to the word family, but are not extra binyanim.

A pupil or disciple

תַּלְמִיד

talmid · One learner.

Pupils or disciples

תַּלְמִידִים

talmidim · More than one learner.

Study or learning

תַּלְמוּד

talmud · The study itself.

The plural for pupils is talmidim, not talmudim. The Biblical pupil and the later rabbinic examples are identified separately in their sources.

Learning to measure

A pupil can learn through measuring and comparing. That teaching connection does not make “learn” and “measure” the same Hebrew root.
