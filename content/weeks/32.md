description: "Week 32 is the strangest book in the Old Testament — and one of its most quietly profound — because of what it never says. Across ten chapters of court intrigue, mortal danger, and last-minute rescue, the name of God is never mentioned once. Set in the Persian court of Ahasuerus (most commonly identified with Xerxes), Esther is the story of the Jews who stayed in the diaspora when the exiles of Ezra and Nehemiah went home: a Jewish orphan crowned queen with her people kept secret, and her cousin Mordecai, who will not bow to the villain Haman. When Haman casts the lot (pur) to destroy every Jew in the empire, Mordecai's charge becomes the book's turning point — 'who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?' — and Esther's answer, 'if I perish, I perish.' What follows is a chain of reversals too precise to be chance: a sleepless king, a forgotten record read at the perfect hour, a villain hanged on the gallows he built. The book that never names God is, from first verse to last, a testimony that He governs history most powerfully when He seems most hidden."
quick_summary: "Week 32 is a lesson in hidden providence — faith for the seasons when God is silent. Esther teaches that the God who is never named is never absent: He governs through 'coincidences' too precise to be chance, positions His people 'for such a time as this,' and turns the designs of the wicked back upon their own heads — 'it was turned to the contrary' (9:1). The study guide traces four Hebrew words — pur (the lot), et (the appointed time), haphak (the great reversal), and malkuth (kingdom) — and reads the rabbis' beloved Megillah, in which even the hidden name of God is heard inside the name Esther. The deliverance is remembered forever in the feast of Purim, so a rescue with no visible miracle would never be forgotten."
