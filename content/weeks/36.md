description: "Week 36 finishes the Psalter, and the ending was built. The Hebrew name for the book is Tehillim — 'Praises' — which has been an odd name all along, since roughly a third of the Psalms are lament. This is the week that earns it: the last five psalms each open and close with 'Praise ye the LORD,' getting shorter and louder until the final verse is a single line. Along the way the week hands you Psalm 103's 'as far as the east is from the west,' Psalm 110's priesthood after the order of Melchizedek, Psalm 119's 176 verses built out of the Hebrew alphabet, and Psalms 116–118 — the Passover Hallel, which is almost certainly the hymn the Savior sang with His Apostles before He went out to Gethsemane."
quick_summary: "The Psalter does not stop; it climbs. Psalm 102 is written by someone at the end of their strength and Psalm 103 answers it with the tenderest line about God's patience in scripture — 'he remembereth that we are dust.' Psalm 110 is the psalm the Savior used to silence the Pharisees, and the only place outside Genesis that names Melchizedek. Psalm 137 weeps by the rivers of Babylon and is kept in the book rather than edited out. And the last word of substance in the Psalms is 'breath' — the same Hebrew word as the breath God breathed into Adam."
