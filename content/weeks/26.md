## Weekly Insights

David's fall begins with a verb of sight. *Vayar* — "and he saw" — the same verb that opens humanity's first fall in Eden. What the man after God's own heart does next, the narrator tells in a chain of cold verbs: he saw, he sent, he took. Israel had asked for a king who would *take* their sons and their fields; in 2 Samuel 11 the beloved king takes the one thing that was never his. And then Nathan tells a story, the trap springs, and David — unlike Saul before him — reaches for no excuse: "I have sinned against the LORD." Out of that ruin come Psalm 51 and a son named *Yedidyah*, "beloved of Yah," who will build God a house carved like Eden and then, wiser than his father and weaker in the end, let a divided heart tear the kingdom in two.
