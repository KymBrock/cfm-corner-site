description: "Every other week of the Old Testament tells the story of a people; this week tells the story of a person — a righteous man, in the land of Uz, caught in undeserved suffering and asking the oldest and hardest question there is: why? The book of Job is honest enough not to give a cheap answer. It raises the problem of innocent suffering and then declines to resolve it — God never tells Job about the scene in heaven that opened the book, and Job never learns why. And yet the man who begins the week having lost everything ends it saying, 'I know that my redeemer liveth' (Job 19:25) and 'now mine eye seeth thee' (Job 42:5). The book does not answer why we suffer. It answers a deeper question: whether God can still be trusted when we do not know why. Latter-day Saints read Job with a unique companion — the Liberty Jail revelations, where the Lord told Joseph Smith, 'Thou art not yet as Job' (D&C 121:10), and 'all these things shall give thee experience... The Son of Man hath descended below them all' (D&C 122:7–8)."
quick_summary: "Week 33 is a lesson in faith for the seasons when God is silent. Job teaches that real faith is not a frozen calm — the wholehearted man of chapter 1 becomes the anguished, arguing man of the middle chapters, and God calls his honest cry right (Job 42:7). The study guide traces four Hebrew words — go'el (the kinsman-Redeemer of 19:25), ha-satan (the accuser), tam (blameless, not sinless), and nacham (the 'repent' of 42:6) — reads the rabbis on whether Job was history or parable and on 'a person is not held responsible for what he says when he is in distress' (Bava Batra 16b), and hears the whirlwind answer Job's 'why' not with an explanation but with an encounter. Job foresaw dimly what we have been given plainly: a living Redeemer who 'will take upon him the pains and the sicknesses of his people' (Alma 7:11)."
