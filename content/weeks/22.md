## Weekly Insights

After Joshua's death, Israel enters its darkest period. The book of Judges records what happens when a covenant people forget their God — not once, but seven times in escalating spirals. Deborah stands as the high-water mark: prophetess, judge, and commander whose Song is among the oldest poetry in the Bible. Gideon's three hundred carry shofars and torches into battle, invoking the fire and sound of Sinai itself. But the arc bends downward. Jephthah's rash vow costs his daughter's life. Samson, consecrated from the womb, systematically breaks every Nazarite vow until the Spirit departs and he doesn't even notice. The treatment of women traces Israel's collapse like a barometer. And after Samson, the deliverers stop coming. "Every man did that which was right in his own eyes." The silence of God at the end of Judges is the loudest sound in the Old Testament — until Samuel hears a voice in the dark.
