description: "Week 34 opens Israel's hymnbook. For a thousand years before Christ, when the covenant people wanted to praise, plead, repent, or remember, they reached for the Psalms — and this week samples the richest part of the collection. Hebrew poetry works unlike our own: it rhymes ideas, not sounds, in lines that echo, balance, contrast, and climb. The reading runs the whole range of the heart before God: the two gateway psalms of the law and the King (1–2), the great creation poems that hear the heavens 'declare the glory of God' (8, 19), the suffering-shepherd-King sequence at the center (Psalm 22's forsaken cry that Jesus prayed from the cross, Psalm 23's 'The Lord is my shepherd,' Psalm 24's 'King of glory'), and the psalm of stillness under a raging world — 'Be still, and know that I am God' (46:10). The Savior quoted the Psalms more than any other book and prayed them as He died; from the begotten Son of Psalm 2 to the pierced hands of Psalm 22, the Psalter is quietly full of Christ."
quick_summary: "Week 34 is a lesson in how to pray — because the Psalms are the prayer book Jesus Himself grew up praying. The study guide reads six key psalms (8, 19, 22, 23, 24, 46) with the voices of the prophets, traces four Hebrew words — torah (instruction), kavod (glory-as-weight), nephesh (the whole living self), and chesed (covenant lovingkindness that pursues us) — and reads the rabbis on David, the psalmist who rose at midnight to praise. A companion interactive piece, 'How Hebrew Poems Are Built,' maps the architecture of these psalms — parallelism, chiasm, inclusio, the acrostic, and wordplays that only live in the Hebrew. The Psalms give us words for every season of the heart, from soaring praise to raw lament, and teach that God is 'a very present help in trouble.'"
