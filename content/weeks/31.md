description: "Week 31 is where the exile ends. The story that closed in ruins at the end of 2 Kings reopens with a decree: the Lord stirs the heart of Cyrus, a Persian king, to send the exiles home to rebuild His house. What follows is restoration in layers — first the altar and the temple foundation, then, after years of opposition, the temple itself when Haggai and Zechariah stir the work and Darius funds it; then Ezra arrives having 'prepared his heart to seek the law of the Lord, and to do it, and to teach'; then Nehemiah rebuilds Jerusalem's wall in fifty-two days against relentless mockery and threat, with prayer in one hand and a trowel in the other; and finally, at the Water Gate, the whole community is re-formed around the word as Ezra reads the law and the Levites 'gave the sense, and caused them to understand.'"
quick_summary: "Week 31 is a lesson about doing the Lord's work amid mockery, threat, and weariness — with prayer in one hand and a trowel (or a sword) in the other. Nehemiah's rallying cry gives the week its theme: 'Let us rise up and build,' and to those who try to lure him off the wall, 'I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down.' The study guide traces the Hebrew of the rebuilding — banah (to build), qum (to rise up), torah (the law), yad (the hand of the Lord), and sofer (Ezra the scribe) — and ends where the restoration lands: an open book and a people who understand it, for 'the joy of the Lord is your strength.'"
