## Overview

Shavuot (שָׁבֻעוֹת, "Weeks") is the fourth of the seven biblical feasts — and the one that bridges the spring and summer seasons. It falls exactly fifty days after Bikkurim (Firstfruits), completing the Counting of the Omer. In Greek it became Pentecost (πεντηκοστή, "fiftieth"). While the Torah describes it as a harvest festival, Jewish tradition identifies it as the anniversary of the most transformative event in Israelite history: the giving of the Torah at Mount Sinai.

For Latter-day Saints, Shavuot holds layers of significance that extend far beyond ancient Israel. The same God who descended on Sinai in fire and smoke ascended a mountain in Galilee to deliver the Sermon on the Mount, then descended in a white robe to the temple at Bountiful. At each station, He gave covenant instruction to His people. And each time, the pattern deepened — from enforced distance to invited touch, from law on stone to law on hearts, from terror to Hosanna.

This article traces that progression: from the original Shavuot at Sinai, through the New Testament Pentecost, to the Sermon on the Mount and the Sermon at the Temple in 3 Nephi — revealing a single divine pattern of covenant-giving that culminates in the presence of the risen Christ.

## The Spring Feast Timeline

The spring *moedim* follow a prophetic sequence:

1. **Passover** (14 Nisan) — Redemption through the blood of the lamb
2. **Unleavened Bread** (15 Nisan) — Separation from sin; Christ in the tomb
3. **Firstfruits / Bikkurim** (16 Nisan / Sunday) — Resurrection; the first sheaf waved
4. **Shavuot / Pentecost** (+50 days, 6 Sivan) — Covenant given; the Spirit descends

The gap between Bikkurim and Shavuot is not empty time. It is the Counting of the Omer — forty-nine days of deliberate, numbered anticipation. Israel counted each day between the first harvest and the full harvest, between resurrection and revelation, between deliverance and covenant.

> "And ye shall count unto you from the morrow after the sabbath, from the day that ye brought the sheaf of the wave offering; seven sabbaths shall be complete: Even unto the morrow after the seventh sabbath shall ye number fifty days; and ye shall offer a new meat offering unto the LORD." (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/lev/23/15/" target="_blank" data-ref="Leviticus 23:15-16">Leviticus 23:15-16</a>)

## The Biblical Foundation

### The Three Names

Scripture gives this feast three names, each revealing a different dimension:

| Name | Hebrew | Meaning | Reference |
|------|--------|---------|-----------|
| **Feast of Weeks** | <a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h7620/kjv/wlc/0-1/" target="_blank">שָׁבֻעוֹת</a> (*Shavuot*) | Seven weeks after Firstfruits | <a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/deu/16/10/" target="_blank" data-ref="Deuteronomy 16:10">Deuteronomy 16:10</a> |
| **Feast of Harvest** | חַג הַקָּצִיר (*Chag HaKatzir*) | Celebrates the wheat harvest | <a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/23/16/" target="_blank" data-ref="Exodus 23:16">Exodus 23:16</a> |
| **Pentecost** | πεντηκοστή (*Pentekoste*) | "Fiftieth" — the Greek name | <a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/act/2/1/" target="_blank" data-ref="Acts 2:1">Acts 2:1</a> |

**A note on "firstfruits":** Numbers 28:26 also calls Shavuot *Yom HaBikkurim* ("Day of Firstfruits"), but this refers to the **wheat** firstfruits offered at the end of the grain harvest — distinct from the **barley** firstfruits (*Bikkurim*) waved during Passover week (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/lev/23/10/" target="_blank" data-ref="Leviticus 23:10-11">Leviticus 23:10-11</a>). The Passover-week Bikkurim — fulfilled in Christ's resurrection — is covered in our [Bikkurim article](/culture/jewish/moedim/bikkurim/).

Unlike Passover and Sukkot, Shavuot has no specific date in the Torah — only a count. It is defined entirely by its relationship to what came before. You cannot observe Shavuot without first counting from Passover. The feast is, by design, a destination reached through sustained anticipation.

### The Pilgrimage Requirement

Shavuot is one of the three pilgrimage festivals (*shalosh regalim*) when every Israelite male was commanded to appear before the Lord at the Temple:

> "Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the LORD thy God in the place which he shall choose; in the feast of unleavened bread, and in the feast of weeks, and in the feast of tabernacles" (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/deu/16/16/" target="_blank" data-ref="Deuteronomy 16:16">Deuteronomy 16:16</a>)

This is why Jerusalem was packed with "devout men, out of every nation under heaven" on the day of Pentecost in Acts 2:5 — they were there for Shavuot.

### The Two Loaves

The distinctive offering of Shavuot is unique among the feasts: two loaves of bread baked with leaven (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/lev/23/17/" target="_blank" data-ref="Leviticus 23:17">Leviticus 23:17</a>). Every other grain offering in the Temple was unleavened. The two leavened loaves are the only exception.

Why leaven? Leaven typically symbolizes sin in scripture (1 Corinthians 5:6-8). But at Shavuot, leavened bread is offered to God — perhaps signaling that the covenant is for a people who are not yet perfected. God accepts what still rises and ferments. The Torah is given not to the sinless but to the willing.

The two loaves have been interpreted as representing:
- Israel and the nations (both invited into covenant)
- The Written Torah and the Oral Torah
- The Old Covenant and the New Covenant

## The First Shavuot: Sinai

### The Timing

While the Torah does not explicitly state that the Ten Commandments were given on Shavuot, the rabbis calculated the timing from the text itself:

| Event | Date | Source |
|-------|------|--------|
| Exodus from Egypt | 15 Nisan (Passover) | Exodus 12:41 |
| Arrival at Sinai | 1 Sivan ("the third month") | Exodus 19:1 |
| Three days of sanctification | 3-5 Sivan | Exodus 19:10-11 |
| God descends on Sinai | 6 Sivan = **Shavuot** | <a href="https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.86b" target="_blank">Talmud, Shabbat 86b</a> |

Fifty days from Passover to Sinai. The same count that defines Shavuot in Leviticus 23 matches the narrative timeline in Exodus 19. The harvest festival and the covenant-giving converge on the same date.

### What Happened at Sinai

The theophany at Sinai was overwhelming:

> "There were thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon the mount, and the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud; so that all the people that was in the camp trembled... mount Sinai was altogether on a smoke, because the LORD descended upon it in fire" (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/19/16/" target="_blank" data-ref="Exodus 19:16-18">Exodus 19:16-18</a>)

Three features define the Sinai encounter:

1. **Enforced distance.** Boundaries were set around the mountain. "Whosoever toucheth the mount shall be surely put to death" (Exodus 19:12). The holiness of God required maximum separation.

2. **Mediated communication.** Moses alone ascended as the designated mediator. The people heard God's voice but could not bear it: "Let not God speak with us, lest we die" (Exodus 20:19). They retreated further, and Moses stood between them and God. The Greek word for mediator — <a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g3316/kjv/tr/0-1/" target="_blank">μεσίτης</a> (*mesites*) — comes from <a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g3319/kjv/tr/0-1/" target="_blank">μέσος</a> (*mesos*), meaning "middle, in the midst." A mediator is literally one who stands in the middle between two parties. Moses' very role at Sinai — ascending to God, descending to the people, standing in the gap — defines what a *mesites* does. In this sense, Moses represents a type and shadow for Jesus Christ. And *mesites* is the exact word the author of Hebrews uses to describe what Christ fulfills: "Jesus the **mediator** [*mesites*] of a new covenant" (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/heb/12/24/" target="_blank" data-ref="Hebrews 12:24">Hebrews 12:24</a>; see also <a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/heb/8/6/" target="_blank" data-ref="Hebrews 8:6">Hebrews 8:6</a>). Moses stood in the *mesos* at Sinai; Christ is the *mesites* of the better covenant — the one who does not merely stand between God and man but *is* both God and man.

3. **Law on stone.** The covenant was inscribed on tablets — external, objective, unchangeable. The people received commandments written by God's finger on dead rock.

The final image of Sinai is distance: "The people stood afar off, and Moses drew near unto the thick darkness where God was" (Exodus 20:21). One man close; the whole nation far away.

### The Rabbinic Shavuot Traditions

Jewish tradition developed rich practices around Shavuot as the anniversary of Torah-giving:

**Tikkun Leil Shavuot** — The practice of staying awake all night studying Torah. According to midrash, Israel overslept on the morning God was to give the Torah, so Moses had to wake them. To atone for this, Jews stay awake all night demonstrating eagerness for God's word (<a href="https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.86b" target="_blank">Talmud, Shabbat 86b</a>).

**The Voice in Seventy Languages** — <a href="https://www.sefaria.org/Shemot_Rabbah.5.9" target="_blank">Exodus Rabbah 5:9</a> teaches that God's voice at Sinai "went forth and was divided into seventy languages, so that all nations heard it." Torah was offered to the whole world.

**God Offered Torah to All Nations** — <a href="https://www.sefaria.org/Sifrei_Devarim.343" target="_blank">Sifre Deuteronomy 343</a> records that God approached every nation before Israel. Each asked what the Torah contained and declined when they heard a commandment that conflicted with their way of life. Only Israel accepted without conditions: "All that the LORD hath spoken we will do" (Exodus 19:8).

**Reading the Book of Ruth** — Ruth is read on Shavuot because she exemplifies voluntary acceptance of Torah. As a Moabite widow, she had every reason to return home. Instead she declared: "Thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God" (Ruth 1:16) — a convert's acceptance of covenant, echoing Israel's acceptance at Sinai.

**Decorating with Greenery** — Synagogues are adorned with flowers and plants, representing Mount Sinai blooming in the desert when God descended.

**Dairy Foods** — Traditional Shavuot meals feature dairy dishes. Multiple explanations exist: Israel had not yet received the kosher laws and so could not prepare meat properly; or the Torah is compared to "milk and honey" (Song of Solomon 4:11); or the two dairy meals plus one meat meal equal the three-day sanctification period.

## The New Testament Shavuot: Pentecost (Acts 2)

Fifty days after Christ's resurrection — on the very day of Shavuot — the Holy Spirit descended on the assembled disciples in Jerusalem.

> "And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place. And suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind, and it filled all the house where they were sitting. And there appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance." (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/act/2/1/" target="_blank" data-ref="Acts 2:1-4">Acts 2:1-4</a>)

The parallels between Sinai and Pentecost are not coincidental — they are fulfillment:

| Sinai (Exodus 19-20) | Pentecost (Acts 2) |
|-----------------------|---------------------|
| Fire descends on the mountain | Tongues of fire descend on each person |
| Voice divided into 70 languages (Exodus Rabbah 5:9) | Disciples speak in every language (Acts 2:4-11) |
| Law written on stone tablets | Law written on hearts by the Spirit (Jeremiah 31:33; 2 Corinthians 3:3) |
| Moses mediates between God and people | The Spirit indwells each believer directly |
| Israel formed as covenant nation | The Church formed as covenant community |
| "The people stood afar off" (Exodus 20:18) | "They were all with one accord in one place" (Acts 2:1) |
| Boundaries enforce distance from God | The Spirit removes the veil — God dwells within |

The prophet Jeremiah had foretold this shift:

> "I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people" (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/jer/31/33/" target="_blank" data-ref="Jeremiah 31:33">Jeremiah 31:33</a>)

Pentecost is the fulfillment of Sinai. What was external becomes internal. What was mediated becomes direct. What was written on stone is written on the heart.

## The Law, the Higher Law, and the Blessing

> "There is a law, irrevocably decreed in heaven before the foundations of this world, upon which all blessings are predicated — And when we obtain any blessing from God, it is by obedience to that law upon which it is predicated." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/130?lang=eng&id=20-21" target="_blank" data-ref="D&C 130:20-21">D&C 130:20-21</a>)

Every commandment has three dimensions — and every commandment carries an inherent blessing. At Sinai, God gave the law: *do not do this.* On the mount in Galilee, Christ revealed the higher law: *do not even harbor the intent.* And in His covenant teaching, He pointed to the action that fulfills the law entirely: *go and do the opposite virtue.* The progression moves from prohibition to purification to consecration — from restraining the hand, to transforming the heart, to actively building the kingdom.

This is not merely good-better-best. It is the architecture of sanctification: each level draws us closer to the character of God.

### The Governing Principle

The movement from law to higher law to covenant action is not a movement from bad to good to better. It is the movement from Sinai to Galilee to Bountiful — from stone to heart to flesh. At each stage, the commandment doesn't change. It deepens. The prohibition remains, but something greater is added: first the restraint of the hand, then the transformation of the heart, then the active pursuit of the virtue the commandment was always pointing toward.

And at every stage, a blessing is irrevocably tied to the law:

> "I, the Lord, am bound when ye do what I say; but when ye do not what I say, ye have no promise." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/82?lang=eng&id=10" target="_blank" data-ref="D&C 82:10">D&C 82:10</a>)

The commandments are not restrictions on freedom. They are the conditions of blessing. Obedience to the law brings temporal safety. Obedience to the higher law brings spiritual transformation. And obedience to the covenant — the full, consecrated, heart-deep response — brings us into the presence of God.

> "Consider on the blessed and happy state of those that keep the commandments of God. For behold, they are blessed in all things, both temporal and spiritual; and if they hold out faithful to the end they are received into heaven, that thereby they may dwell with God in a state of never-ending happiness." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/2?lang=eng&id=41" target="_blank" data-ref="Mosiah 2:41">Mosiah 2:41</a>)

## The Mountain of the Lord: A Temple Progression

### Ascending and Descending

The biblical pattern of meeting God consistently involves a mountain — and the mountain is consistently identified with the temple. Isaiah's vision captures both:

> "And it shall come to pass in the last days, that the mountain of the LORD's house shall be established in the top of the mountains, and shall be exalted above the hills; and all nations shall flow unto it." (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/isa/2/2/" target="_blank" data-ref="Isaiah 2:2">Isaiah 2:2</a>)

Mountain and temple are synonymous. To ascend the mountain is to ascend to God's presence. To descend is to carry His covenant back to the world.

John W. Welch, in his landmark study *The Sermon at the Temple and the Sermon on the Mount* (FARMS, 1990), argued that the Sermon on the Mount is not merely ethical teaching but a **temple text** — a structured progression through the stages of approaching, covenanting with, and entering the presence of God. Welch identified **25 stages in the Sermon on the Mount that correspond to temple themes**: from initial preparation and purification (the Beatitudes), through covenant instruction (the antitheses and teachings on prayer, fasting, and almsgiving), to the culminating encounter with God and the charge to build on the rock.

When this same sermon is delivered at the temple in Bountiful (3 Nephi 12-14), Welch found the temple elements **doubled** — approximately 50 identifiable parallels — because the temple setting is explicit and the resurrected Christ is present in person.

### Three Mountains, One Progression

The God of Israel gave covenant instruction at three mountains. Each encounter escalated in proximity and intimacy:

**1. Mount Sinai (Exodus 19-20) — The Distant Mountain**
- God descends in fire, smoke, and earthquake
- Boundaries enforced: touch the mountain and die
- The people stand "afar off" and beg for a mediator
- Moses alone ascends
- Law given on stone — external, objective, unbending

**2. The Mountain in Galilee (Matthew 5-7) — The Teaching Mountain**
- Jesus ascends; disciples come to Him (no boundary mentioned)
- He sits — the rabbinic posture of authoritative instruction
- He teaches directly; no intermediary needed
- The crowd is "astonished" — awe replaces terror
- The Law is interpreted from within: "Ye have heard... but I say unto you"
- Fulfillment is future: "till all be fulfilled" (Matthew 5:18)

**3. The Temple at Bountiful (3 Nephi 11-12) — The Temple of Presence**
- Christ descends in a white robe and stands "in the midst of them" (3 Nephi 11:8)
- No boundary. Instead: "Come forth unto me, that ye may feel the prints of the nails" (3 Nephi 11:14)
- The multitude goes forth "one by one" — every person touches Him
- Corporate response: "Hosanna! Blessed be the name of the Most High God!" (3 Nephi 11:17)
- Fulfillment is past: "In me it hath all been fulfilled" (3 Nephi 12:18)
- Baptism established immediately; the Spirit follows (3 Nephi 19)

### The Escalating Proximity

| Feature | Sinai | Sermon on Mount | Bountiful |
|---------|-------|-----------------|-----------|
| **God's approach** | Descends in fire/smoke | Jesus ascends mountain | Christ descends in white robe, stands among them |
| **Touch** | Death for touching the mount | No command | "Come forth... feel the prints" |
| **People's response** | Fell back; stood afar off | Astonished | Fell down; rose; worshipped |
| **Contact with God** | Moses only | No record of touch | One by one, ALL touched Him |
| **Intermediary** | Moses (essential) | None (Jesus teaches directly) | None; the multitude comes directly |
| **Fulfillment** | Law given (to be fulfilled) | "Till all be fulfilled" (future) | "In me it hath all been fulfilled" (past) |
| **Perfection standard** | N/A | "Your Father in heaven" (Matthew 5:48) | "Even as I, or your Father" (3 Nephi 12:48) |
| **Lord's Prayer** | N/A | "Thy kingdom come" (anticipated) | "Thy kingdom come" omitted (kingdom present) |
| **Covenant ordinance** | Follows in Exodus 24 | No immediate ordinance | Baptism established immediately |

The pattern is a single movement: from maximum enforced distance to maximum invited proximity. The mountain of the Lord draws nearer until it stands among us in the flesh.

### The Beatitudes and Temple Entry

Welch's temple reading illuminates the Beatitudes in particular. Rather than general moral ideals, they function as **conditions of approach** — the qualifications for ascending into the presence of God:

- "Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God" (Matthew 5:8) — the culminating promise is theophany, seeing God face to face
- "Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God" (Matthew 5:9) — divine sonship/daughtership, the covenant identity received in the temple

The Hebrew behind "blessed" — <a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h835/kjv/wlc/0-1/" target="_blank">אַשְׁרֵי</a> (*ashrei*) — is not passive contentment but active recognition: "How blessed is the one who walks!" It describes a person already in covenant motion. Psalm 119 opens with *ashrei* **doubled** — two declarations of blessedness before a single commandment is given (Psalm 119:1-2).

At Bountiful, Christ mirrors this pattern exactly. Before the formal Beatitudes begin, He opens with **two** preliminary blessings:

> "Blessed are ye if ye shall give heed unto the words of these twelve whom I have chosen" (3 Nephi 12:1)
> "And again, more blessed are they who shall believe in your words" (3 Nephi 12:2)

Then the Beatitudes proper begin at 3 Nephi 12:3. The doubled *ashrei* opening of Psalm 119 — the great Torah psalm — reappears at the temple in Bountiful, where the resurrected Christ delivers the fulfilled Torah to His covenant people.

## The Bountiful Visit as Shavuot

### The Nephites and the Law of Moses

The Book of Mormon is explicit: the Nephites observed the Law of Moses throughout their history. This is not a minor detail — it means they kept the feasts, the sacrifices, and the appointed times (*moedim*):

> "And we did observe to keep the judgments, and the statutes, and the commandments of the Lord in all things, **according to the law of Moses**." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/5?lang=eng&id=10" target="_blank" data-ref="2 Nephi 5:10">2 Nephi 5:10</a>)

> "They observed to keep the law of Moses and the **sabbath day** holy unto the Lord." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jarom/1?lang=eng&id=5" target="_blank" data-ref="Jarom 1:5">Jarom 1:5</a>)

> "They did keep the law of Moses; for it was expedient that they should keep the law of Moses as yet, for it was not all fulfilled. But notwithstanding the law of Moses, they did look forward to the coming of Christ, considering that **the law of Moses was a type of his coming**." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/25?lang=eng&id=15" target="_blank" data-ref="Alma 25:15">Alma 25:15</a>)

> "The people did observe to keep the commandments of the Lord; and they were strict in observing **the ordinances of God, according to the law of Moses**; for they were taught to keep the law of Moses until it should be fulfilled." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/30?lang=eng&id=3" target="_blank" data-ref="Alma 30:3">Alma 30:3</a>)

Even shortly before Christ's coming, the text confirms ongoing observance: some preached "that it was no more expedient to observe the law of Moses. Now in this thing they did err, having not understood the scriptures" (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/1?lang=eng&id=24" target="_blank" data-ref="3 Nephi 1:24">3 Nephi 1:24</a>). The law was still in force. The feasts were still being observed.

### The Typological Pattern

If the Nephites kept the Law of Moses — including the three pilgrimage festivals — then the faithful among them would have been gathered at the temple for Shavuot in the weeks following Passover season. The destruction recorded in 3 Nephi 8 occurred "in the thirty and fourth year, in the first month, on the fourth day of the month" (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/8?lang=eng&id=5" target="_blank" data-ref="3 Nephi 8:5">3 Nephi 8:5</a>) — Passover season. Fifty days later would place the Shavuot observance squarely in the window when the multitude was gathered "round about the temple" at Bountiful (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/11?lang=eng&id=1" target="_blank" data-ref="3 Nephi 11:1">3 Nephi 11:1</a>).

On the other side of the world, the apostles were gathered at the temple in Jerusalem for the same feast — Pentecost — when the Spirit descended in Acts 2.

The pattern of deliverance followed by covenant-giving repeats with striking precision:

| Pattern | Old Testament | New Testament | Book of Mormon |
|---------|---------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Deliverance** | Passover / Red Sea | Christ's death and resurrection | Three days of darkness; destruction of the wicked |
| **Waiting period** | 50 days (Omer count) | 50 days (Acts 1:3 — 40 days with disciples + 10 days waiting) | Darkness lifts; the faithful gather at the temple — likely for Shavuot |
| **Covenant event** | Torah given at Sinai | Holy Spirit at Pentecost | Christ appears at Bountiful; teaches; baptizes |
| **Fire** | Fire on the mountain | Tongues of fire on each person | 3 Nephi 19:14 — "encircled about as if it were by fire" |
| **Result** | Israel formed as covenant nation | Church formed as covenant community | Nephite covenant community renewed |

Krister Stendahl, the Swedish theologian and New Testament scholar, identified 3 Nephi 19 specifically as a Pentecost scene — noting the high-priestly prayer style (paralleling John 17), the descent of the Spirit, and the fire that encircles the disciples (BYU Religious Studies Center, 1978).

### What the Resurrected Christ Changed

The differences between the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7) and the Sermon at the Temple (3 Nephi 12-14) are not scribal errors. They are post-resurrection theology. The same Lawgiver speaks — but He speaks *after* the Atonement is complete:

**Fulfillment tense:**
- Matthew 5:18: "Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, **till all be fulfilled**" — future
- 3 Nephi 12:18: "One jot nor one tittle hath not passed away from the law, but **in me it hath all been fulfilled**" — past

**The perfection standard:**
- Matthew 5:48: "Be ye therefore perfect, even as **your Father** which is in heaven is perfect"
- 3 Nephi 12:48: "I would that ye should be perfect **even as I, or your Father** who is in heaven is perfect" — the resurrected Christ adds Himself to the standard

**The Lord's Prayer:**
- Matthew 6:10: "**Thy kingdom come.** Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven"
- 3 Nephi 13:10: "Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven" — "Thy kingdom come" is absent; the kingdom is already present in the person of the risen Lord

**The antithesis formula:**
- Matthew 5:21: "Ye have **heard** that it was said by them of old time..."
- 3 Nephi 12:21: "Ye have heard that it hath been said by them of old time, **and it is also written before you**..."

As Welch observed, the Nephites relied on the written law (the plates of brass), not oral tradition. The formula was adapted for a people who knew Torah through text, not through rabbinic teaching (*The Sermon at the Temple and the Sermon on the Mount*, 1990).

**"Filled with the Holy Ghost":**
- Matthew 5:6: "Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they **shall be filled**"
- 3 Nephi 12:6: "Blessed are all they who do hunger and thirst after righteousness, for they shall be **filled with the Holy Ghost**"

The specific Pentecostal gift is named. What was implicit at Sinai (the Spirit manifest in fire and voice), promised at the Sermon on the Mount (filled), and delivered at Acts 2 (tongues of fire), is here made explicit: the filling is the Holy Ghost.

## The Wheat Harvest in Restoration Scripture

Shavuot is the wheat harvest. And the imagery of wheat, sheaves, sickles, and garners saturates Restoration scripture in ways that connect directly to this ancient feast.

### "The Field Is White Already to Harvest"

The Lord's most repeated phrase in early Restoration revelation is a harvest call:

> "Behold, the field is white already to harvest; therefore, whoso desireth to reap, let him thrust in his sickle with his might, and reap while the day lasts, that he may treasure up for his soul everlasting salvation in the kingdom of God." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/4?lang=eng&id=4" target="_blank" data-ref="D&C 4:4">D&C 4:4</a>; repeated in D&C 6:3, 11:3, 12:3, 14:3, 33:3, 7)

This language comes from Christ Himself. At Jacob's well, speaking to His disciples, Jesus said:

> "Say not ye, There are yet four months, and then cometh harvest? behold, I say unto you, Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields; for they are white already to harvest." (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/jhn/4/35/" target="_blank" data-ref="John 4:35">John 4:35</a>)

At Shavuot, the wheat fields were indeed white and ripe. The feast marked the culmination of the grain harvest — the moment when laborers thrust in their sickles and gathered sheaves into garners. When the Lord uses this language in the Doctrine and Covenants, He is invoking the Shavuot harvest as the image for missionary gathering.

### Ammon and the Sheaves

The sons of Mosiah spent fourteen years among the Lamanites. When they reunited, Ammon's rejoicing is saturated with harvest language:

> "Behold, the field was ripe, and blessed are ye, for ye did thrust in the sickle, and did reap with your might, yea, all the day long did ye labor; and behold the number of your **sheaves**! And they shall be gathered into the **garners**, that they are not wasted." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/26?lang=eng&id=5" target="_blank" data-ref="Alma 26:5">Alma 26:5</a>)

> "But behold, they are in the hands of the **Lord of the harvest**, and they are his; and he will raise them up at the last day." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/26?lang=eng&id=7" target="_blank" data-ref="Alma 26:7">Alma 26:7</a>)

Even at their departure, the harvest framing is explicit: the sons of Mosiah "separated themselves and departed one from another, trusting in the Lord that they should meet again at the close of their **harvest**" (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/17?lang=eng&id=13" target="_blank" data-ref="Alma 17:13">Alma 17:13</a>).

The converts are sheaves. The missionaries are laborers. The gathering place is the garner. And the Lord of the harvest is Christ — the same God who instituted Shavuot as the feast of ingathering.

### Laden with Sheaves

The sheaves-on-the-back image appears repeatedly in the Doctrine and Covenants as the promised reward of faithful missionary labor:

> "Thrust in your sickle with all your soul, and your sins are forgiven you, and you shall be **laden with sheaves upon your back**, for the laborer is worthy of his hire." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/31?lang=eng&id=5" target="_blank" data-ref="D&C 31:5">D&C 31:5</a>)

> "If ye are faithful ye shall be **laden with many sheaves**, and crowned with honor, and glory, and immortality, and eternal life." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/75?lang=eng&id=5" target="_blank" data-ref="D&C 75:5">D&C 75:5</a>)

At Shavuot, Israelite farmers literally carried sheaves of wheat to the Temple as offerings. The image of a missionary laden with sheaves on his back — souls gathered to Christ — mirrors the Shavuot pilgrim arriving at the House of the Lord with the fruits of his harvest.

### Wheat and Tares: The Final Harvest

The Lord's explanation of the wheat-and-tares parable in D&C 86 places the entire latter-day gathering in harvest language:

> "Let the wheat and the tares grow together until the harvest is fully ripe; then ye shall first gather out the **wheat** from among the tares, and after the gathering of the wheat, behold and lo, the tares are bound in bundles, and the field remaineth to be burned." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/86?lang=eng&id=7" target="_blank" data-ref="D&C 86:7">D&C 86:7</a>)

> "I must gather together my people, according to the parable of the wheat and the tares, that the **wheat may be secured in the garners** to possess eternal life, and be crowned with celestial glory." (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/101?lang=eng&id=65" target="_blank" data-ref="D&C 101:65">D&C 101:65</a>)

The gathering of Israel is, in the Lord's own imagery, a wheat harvest. And the wheat harvest is Shavuot — the feast that celebrates both the ingathering of grain and the giving of the covenant that makes God's people His own.

### The Pattern

| Shavuot Element | Restoration Parallel |
|-----------------|----------------------|
| Wheat fields white and ripe | "The field is white already to harvest" (D&C 4:4) |
| Laborers thrust in sickles | Missionaries called to "thrust in your sickle" (D&C 31:5) |
| Sheaves gathered to garners | Converts gathered to Zion / temples |
| Sheaves carried to the Temple | Missionaries "laden with sheaves upon your back" (D&C 75:5) |
| Wheat separated from chaff | Wheat gathered from tares (D&C 86:7) |
| Offering of two leavened loaves | A people not yet perfected, offered to God |
| Shavuot = covenant renewal | Gathering culminates in temple covenants |

The harvest imagery of Restoration scripture is not incidental. It draws from the same well that feeds Shavuot: the conviction that God's people are His crop, that the covenant is the garner, and that the laborers who gather are doing the Lord's own work in the Lord's own season.

## From Stone to Flesh: The Arc of Shavuot

The entire Shavuot pattern traces a single divine movement:

**Sinai:** God writes His law on stone and delivers it through a mediator. The people cannot bear His presence. They stand afar off.

**The Sermon on the Mount:** The Lawgiver Himself sits among the people and interprets His own law. Distance is reduced. Fear becomes astonishment. But fulfillment remains future — the law is still operative, the kingdom still anticipated.

**Pentecost (Acts 2):** The Spirit descends and writes the law on hearts. No mediator, no tablets, no mountain. God dwells within each person. The promise of Jeremiah 31:33 is realized.

**Bountiful:** The resurrected Christ descends, stands among His people, and invites every single one to touch Him. The law is fulfilled. The kingdom is present. The veil is gone. Where Sinai said "touch the mountain and die," the risen Lord says "come forth unto me, that ye may feel."

This is the arc of Shavuot: from stone to flesh, from distance to embrace, from "let not God speak with us, lest we die" to "Hosanna! Blessed be the name of the Most High God."

## Modern Observance

### Jewish Practice Today

Modern Jewish observance of Shavuot includes:

- **Tikkun Leil Shavuot** — All-night Torah study sessions, demonstrating eagerness to receive God's word
- **Reading the Ten Commandments** — Synagogues chant the Decalogue during Shavuot morning services, with the congregation standing
- **Reading the Book of Ruth** — A story of voluntary covenant acceptance, mirroring Israel's choice at Sinai
- **Decorating with flowers and greenery** — Representing Sinai blooming in the desert
- **Dairy meals** — Traditional foods including cheesecake, blintzes, and other dairy dishes
- **Confirmation ceremonies** — In Reform Judaism, Shavuot is the day for confirming young people in their faith

### Latter-day Saint Resonance

For Latter-day Saints, the Shavuot pattern echoes through our own covenant life:

**Temple worship** follows the Sinai-to-Bountiful progression. We prepare (sanctification), receive instruction (covenant law), make covenants (the stipulations), and are promised entry into God's presence (the culminating blessing). The temple *is* the mountain of the Lord — the place where we ascend to meet God and descend carrying His covenant.

**The sacrament** renews covenant weekly — a personal Shavuot rhythm. We do not celebrate Torah-giving once a year but recommit to the covenant every Sabbath, taking upon ourselves the name of Christ and promising to always remember Him.

**Scripture study** mirrors Tikkun Leil Shavuot. The practice of daily, sustained engagement with God's word is the Latter-day Saint version of staying awake for Torah. We cannot coast on last year's study any more than Israel could store yesterday's manna.

**The Restoration itself** follows the Shavuot pattern. The First Vision (theophany), the translation of the Book of Mormon (new covenant text), and the outpouring of the Spirit at the Kirtland Temple dedication (D&C 109-110) mirror Sinai, Torah-giving, and Pentecost — the same God repeating the same pattern in the latter days.

## An Invitation

Shavuot reminds us that deliverance is only the beginning. God did not free Israel from Egypt simply to wander. He freed them for covenant — for relationship, for instruction, for the privilege of ascending His mountain and hearing His voice.

The counting of the Omer teaches us that the space between deliverance and covenant is not empty — it is preparation. We are always in that space: between what God has already done for us and what He invites us to become. The fifty days are for readying the heart.

And the three mountains remind us where the story ends: not at a distance, not through a mediator, not in terror — but face to face, hand to wound, one by one, with the resurrected Lord standing in our midst, saying: *Come forth unto me.*
