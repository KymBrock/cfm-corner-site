## When Hebrew Wants to Be Emphatic

In the last two lessons, you learned that Hebrew embeds pronouns directly into verbs and prepositions. The verb שָׁמַרְתִּי (*shamarti*) already means "**I** kept" — the suffix -תִּי carries the "I." The preposition לוֹ (*lo*) already means "to **him**" — the suffix -וֹ carries the "him."

So why would Hebrew need separate words for "I" and "you" and "he"?

The answer: **emphasis**.

When Hebrew wants to stress *who* is acting — to draw attention to the subject rather than just noting it — it uses **independent personal pronouns**. These are standalone words that can appear alongside a verb or stand alone in a sentence.

In [Exodus 15:2](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/15/2/), Moses sings after crossing the Red Sea:

<div style="background: rgba(74,107,82,0.08); border-left: 4px solid #4a6b52; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin: 20px 0;">

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.4em; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 12px; text-align: right;">עָזִּי וְזִמְרָת יָהּ וַיְהִי־לִי לִישׁוּעָה זֶה אֵלִי וְאַנְוֵהוּ</p>

*"The LORD is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation: **he** is my God, and I will prepare him an habitation."*

</div>

The word <span dir="rtl" lang="he">**זֶה**</span> (*zeh*, "this one") points emphatically: "*This* is my God." Moses isn't just noting a fact — he's declaring it. The independent pronoun adds weight.

This lesson introduces the full set of Hebrew personal pronouns — the words for "I," "you," "he," "she," "we," and "they" — and shows when and why Hebrew reaches for them.

## When Hebrew Uses Independent Pronouns

Hebrew verbs already encode who is acting. So when does Hebrew add an independent pronoun? Three main situations:

### 1. Emphasis and Contrast

When the speaker wants to stress *who* is acting — especially in contrast to someone else — an independent pronoun appears.

In [Exodus 14:4](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/14/4/), God announces His purpose in the final confrontation:

<div style="background: rgba(30,58,79,0.08); border-left: 4px solid #1e3a4f; border-radius: 8px; padding: 16px 20px; margin-bottom: 20px;">

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.2em; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 8px; text-align: right;">וְיָדְעוּ מִצְרַיִם כִּי־<strong>אֲנִי</strong> יְהוָה</p>

*"And the Egyptians shall know that **I** am the LORD."*

</div>

The verb וְיָדְעוּ (*veyad'u*, "and they shall know") already identifies the Egyptians as the subject. But when it comes to identifying *who* the LORD is, the independent pronoun <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אֲנִי</span> appears. This is emphatic: not Pharaoh, not the Egyptian gods — **I** am the LORD.

This formula — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אֲנִי יְהוָה</span> (*ani YHWH*, "I am the LORD") — appears over 160 times in the Hebrew Bible. It is a declaration of identity, and the pronoun carries the weight of that declaration.

### 2. Verbless (Nominal) Sentences

Hebrew can form complete sentences without verbs. These are called **nominal sentences** or **verbless clauses**. When the subject is a pronoun, the independent form must appear.

In [Exodus 15:2](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/15/2/):

<div style="background: rgba(74,107,82,0.08); border-left: 4px solid #4a6b52; border-radius: 8px; padding: 16px 20px; margin-bottom: 20px;">

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.2em; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 8px; text-align: right;">זֶה אֵלִי וְאַנְוֵהוּ</p>

*"**This** is my God, and I will glorify him."*

</div>

The word <span dir="rtl" lang="he">זֶה</span> ([*zeh*](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h2088/kjv/wlc/0-1/)) is the masculine demonstrative pronoun meaning "this." There is no verb "is" in Hebrew — the sentence <span dir="rtl" lang="he">זֶה אֵלִי</span> simply places two elements side by side: "This — my God." The pronoun carries the entire statement.

<div style="background: rgba(213,169,60,0.12); border-left: 4px solid #d5a93c; border-radius: 8px; padding: 16px 20px; margin-bottom: 20px;">

**Why is there no "to be" verb?**

Hebrew does have a verb meaning "to be" — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">הָיָה</span> ([*hayah*](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h1961/kjv/wlc/0-1/)) — but it is used sparingly, primarily to indicate past or future states. In present-tense statements, Hebrew typically leaves the copula ("is/am/are") unexpressed.

This linguistic feature carries profound theological weight. The root ה-י-ה (*h-y-h*, "to be/exist") is the very root from which the Divine Name <span dir="rtl" lang="he">יְהוָה</span> (YHWH) derives. When God revealed His name to Moses at the burning bush, He declared:

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.15em; text-align: center; margin: 12px 0;"><strong>אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה</strong></p>

*"I AM THAT I AM"* — or more precisely, *"I Will Be What I Will Be"* ([Exodus 3:14](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/3/14/))

The word <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אֶהְיֶה</span> (*ehyeh*) is the first-person singular imperfect of <span dir="rtl" lang="he">הָיָה</span> — "I am" or "I will be." The Divine Name itself *is* the verb of existence.

Some Jewish traditions suggest that Hebrew's reluctance to use the "to be" verb in present-tense statements reflects a deep reverence: **existence itself belongs to God**. To declare that something simply "is" invokes the very Name that embodies Being. The absent verb becomes a kind of holy silence — a grammatical acknowledgment that all existence flows from the One who said <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אֶהְיֶה</span>.

When Moses sings <span dir="rtl" lang="he">זֶה אֵלִי</span> — "This [is] my God" — the missing "is" points to the God whose Name *is* "IS."

</div>

### 3. After Particles and for Clarification

Certain Hebrew particles require independent pronouns. The particle <span dir="rtl" lang="he">הִנֵּה</span> ([*hinneh*](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h2009/kjv/wlc/0-1/), "behold") often appears with a pronoun:

In [Exodus 14:17](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/14/17/):

<div style="background: rgba(30,58,79,0.08); border-left: 4px solid #1e3a4f; border-radius: 8px; padding: 16px 20px; margin-bottom: 20px;">

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.2em; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 8px; text-align: right;">וַ<strong>אֲנִי</strong> הִנְנִי מְחַזֵּק אֶת־לֵב מִצְרַיִם</p>

*"And **I**, behold, I will harden the hearts of the Egyptians."*

</div>

Here <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אֲנִי</span> appears alongside <span dir="rtl" lang="he">הִנְנִי</span> (*hineni*, "behold me" — itself <span dir="rtl" lang="he">הִנֵּה</span> + the 1cs suffix). The double emphasis — "And **I**, behold **I**" — underscores that God Himself is acting.

<div style="background: rgba(195,124,60,0.1); border-left: 4px solid #c37c3c; border-radius: 8px; padding: 16px 20px; margin-bottom: 20px;">

**The "Hardening" Controversy: What Does the Hebrew Reveal?**

This verse has troubled readers for millennia. Did God override Pharaoh's agency by hardening his heart? The Joseph Smith Translation changes several passages to read that *Pharaoh* hardened his own heart (see [JST Exodus 4:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/jst/jst-ex/4?lang=eng); [7:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/jst/jst-ex/7?lang=eng); [9:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/jst/jst-ex/9?lang=eng)), suggesting God *permitted* rather than *caused* the hardening.

The Hebrew offers important nuance. Three different verbs describe Pharaoh's heart throughout Exodus:

| Hebrew | Root | Literal Meaning | Usage Pattern |
|--------|------|-----------------|---------------|
| <span dir="rtl" lang="he">כָּבֵד</span> | [כ-ב-ד](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h3513/kjv/wlc/0-1/) | "heavy, weighty" | Pharaoh's heart *was heavy* (his own state) |
| <span dir="rtl" lang="he">חָזַק</span> | [ח-ז-ק](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h2388/kjv/wlc/0-1/) | "strong, firm" | Heart *was strengthened* or *made firm* |
| <span dir="rtl" lang="he">קָשָׁה</span> | [ק-ש-ה](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h7185/kjv/wlc/0-1/) | "hard, difficult" | Heart *became hard* |

The word in our verse — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">מְחַזֵּק</span> ([*mechazzeq*](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h2388/kjv/wlc/0-1/)) — is a Piel participle of <span dir="rtl" lang="he">חָזַק</span>. The Piel stem often indicates *intensification* or *causation*, but the root <span dir="rtl" lang="he">חָזַק</span> fundamentally means **"to strengthen"** or **"to make firm."**

Consider the pattern in Exodus — tracking *who* does the hardening:

| Verse | Hebrew | Action |
|-------|--------|--------|
| [8:15](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/8/15/) | <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַיַּכְבֵּד **פַּרְעֹה** אֶת־לִבּוֹ</span> | **Pharaoh** made his heart heavy |
| [8:32](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/8/32/) | <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַיַּכְבֵּד **פַּרְעֹה** אֶת־לִבּוֹ</span> | **Pharaoh** made his heart heavy |
| [9:12](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/9/12/) | <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַיְחַזֵּק **יְהוָה** אֶת־לֵב פַּרְעֹה</span> | **First explicit**: LORD strengthened |
| [9:34](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/9/34/) | <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַיַּכְבֵּד **פַּרְעֹה** אֶת־לִבּוֹ</span> | **Pharaoh** made his heart heavy (again!) |
| [10:20](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/10/20/) | <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַיְחַזֵּק **יְהוָה**</span> | LORD strengthened |

The text shows Pharaoh actively hardening his own heart *before* God ever does — and even *after* the first divine hardening (9:12), Pharaoh still hardens his own heart again (9:34). The pattern is interwoven, not sequential, but the priority is clear: Pharaoh chooses first.

One reading: God did not *create* Pharaoh's resistance but *strengthened* (<span dir="rtl" lang="he">חָזַק</span>) what Pharaoh had already made heavy (<span dir="rtl" lang="he">כָּבֵד</span>). When Pharaoh repeatedly chose defiance, God gave him the strength to persist in his own decision — the terrifying gift of being allowed to become what he insisted on being.

The double pronoun <span dir="rtl" lang="he">וַאֲנִי הִנְנִי</span> ("And **I**, behold **I**") may thus carry a different weight: not "I will force him against his will" but "I declare that I will confirm him in the path he has chosen." Agency remains — but so do consequences.

The Hebrew text itself preserves the moral clarity: Pharaoh made his heart *heavy* (<span dir="rtl" lang="he">כָּבֵד</span>); God made it *strong* (<span dir="rtl" lang="he">חָזַק</span>). The verbs tell the story.

</div>

## Pronouns in the Song of the Sea

This week's reading includes the Song of Moses (Exodus 15:1-18), one of the oldest and most celebrated poems in the Hebrew Bible. It is rich with personal pronouns.

### "I Will Sing" — The Opening Declaration

<div style="background: rgba(74,107,82,0.08); border-left: 4px solid #4a6b52; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin: 20px 0;">

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.3em; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 12px; text-align: right;">אָשִׁירָה לַיהוָה כִּי־גָאֹה גָּאָה</p>

*"**I will sing** unto the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously."* — [Exodus 15:1](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/15/1/)

</div>

The verb <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אָשִׁירָה</span> ([*ashirah*](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h7891/kjv/wlc/0-1/)) is a 1cs cohortative form — "let me sing" or "I will sing." The prefix אָ- marks first person singular. No independent pronoun is needed because the verb itself carries the "I."

But notice what happens two verses later:

### "He Is My God" — Emphatic Declaration

<div style="background: rgba(74,107,82,0.08); border-left: 4px solid #4a6b52; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin: 20px 0;">

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.3em; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 12px; text-align: right;">עָזִּי וְזִמְרָת יָהּ וַיְהִי־לִי לִישׁוּעָה <strong>זֶה</strong> אֵלִי וְאַנְוֵהוּ אֱלֹהֵי אָבִי וַאֲרֹמְמֶנְהוּ</p>

*"The LORD is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation: **this** is my God, and I will prepare him an habitation; my father's God, and I will exalt him."* — [Exodus 15:2](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/15/2/)

</div>

The demonstrative <span dir="rtl" lang="he">זֶה</span> (*zeh*, "this one") functions like a pronoun here, pointing emphatically: "**This** is my God!" Moses isn't merely identifying deity — he's declaring allegiance. The pronoun carries the weight of personal testimony.

### "Who Is Like Thee?" — Rhetorical Question

<div style="background: rgba(74,107,82,0.08); border-left: 4px solid #4a6b52; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin: 20px 0;">

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.3em; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 12px; text-align: right;">מִי־כָמֹכָה בָּאֵלִם יְהוָה מִי כָּמֹכָה נֶאְדָּר בַּקֹּדֶשׁ</p>

*"Who is like unto **thee**, O LORD, among the gods? Who is like **thee**, glorious in holiness?"* — [Exodus 15:11](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/15/11/)

</div>

The word <span dir="rtl" lang="he">כָמֹכָה</span> ([*kamokha*](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h3644/kjv/wlc/0-1/)) is the preposition כְּ (*ke-*, "like") + the 2ms pronominal suffix: "like-you." This is the suffix form, not the independent pronoun — but it appears in a context of intense emphasis. The rhetorical question expects the answer: "No one."

**The pattern:** Independent pronouns are **standalone words** that function as sentence subjects or add emphasis. Pronominal suffixes are **attached endings** that modify verbs, prepositions, and nouns.

- <span dir="rtl" lang="he">**אֲנִי** שָׁמַרְתִּי</span> (*ani shamarti*) — "**I** kept" (emphatic — *I* kept, not someone else)
- <span dir="rtl" lang="he">שָׁמַרְתִּי</span> (*shamarti*) — "I kept" (standard — the suffix carries the subject)

Both sentences mean "I kept," but the first emphasizes *who* did the keeping.

## Pronouns and Divine Self-Revelation

One of the most theologically significant uses of independent pronouns appears in divine self-declarations. When God reveals His identity, He often uses the emphatic pronoun.

### "I Am the LORD" — The Covenant Formula

<div style="background: rgba(30,58,79,0.1); border-left: 4px solid #1e3a4f; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin: 20px 0;">

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.4em; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 12px; text-align: right;"><strong>אֲנִי</strong> יְהוָה</p>

*"**I** am the LORD."*

</div>

This two-word phrase — <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אֲנִי יְהוָה</span> (*ani YHWH*) — is the fundamental divine self-identification in the Hebrew Bible. It appears in:

- [Exodus 6:2](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/6/2/) — God's revelation to Moses
- [Exodus 6:6-8](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/6/6/) — The four promises of redemption (each ending with this formula)
- [Exodus 14:4, 18](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/14/4/) — The purpose of the Red Sea deliverance
- Throughout Leviticus — The refrain of holiness legislation

The pronoun is essential. God doesn't say <span dir="rtl" lang="he">יְהוָה אֲנִי</span> (predicate first) but <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אֲנִי יְהוָה</span> (subject first). The emphasis falls on the **I** — the personal God who speaks, acts, and relates.

### "I Am the LORD Your God" — The Decalogue Opening

<div style="background: rgba(74,107,82,0.08); border-left: 4px solid #4a6b52; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin: 20px 0;">

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size: 1.3em; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 12px; text-align: right;"><strong>אָנֹכִי</strong> יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ אֲשֶׁר הוֹצֵאתִיךָ מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם</p>

*"**I** am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt."* — [Exodus 20:2](https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/exo/20/2/)

</div>

Here the longer form <span dir="rtl" lang="he">אָנֹכִי</span> (*anokhi*) appears — the more formal, emphatic "I." The Ten Commandments don't begin with a command but with a declaration of identity and relationship. Before the law comes the Lawgiver; before the imperatives comes the indicative of grace.

## What Comes Next

You now have the complete pronoun system of Biblical Hebrew:

- ✅ **Pronominal suffixes** — attached to verbs (Lesson 10), prepositions (Lesson 11)
- ✅ **Independent pronouns** — standalone words for emphasis and nominal sentences (this lesson)
- ✅ **Demonstrative pronouns** — "this" and "that"

With this foundation, you can identify *who* is acting in almost any Hebrew sentence — whether the pronoun is embedded in a verb, attached to a preposition, or standing alone for emphasis.

The next step takes us into the heart of the Hebrew verb: the **Qatal** (perfect) conjugation — the form that describes completed action. We've already seen its suffixes; now we'll learn how the whole system works.
