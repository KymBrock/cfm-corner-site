<h2 style="text-align: center;">LDS-Specific Connections</h2>

<p style="text-align: center; color: #666; font-style: italic; margin-bottom: 2rem;">The return from exile as the Bible&rsquo;s great pattern of gathering, restoration, and providence</p>

<p>For Latter-day Saints, the Persian period is not a distant footnote &mdash; it is a working model of how God gathers His people, prepares kings and nations for His purposes, and rebuilds what has fallen. The Restoration reads its own mission in these very terms: a gathering of scattered Israel, a building of temples, a re-centering on scripture, and a divine hand moving quietly through the politics of empires. This page draws those threads together.</p>

<br>

<hr>

<br>

<h3>Cyrus and the Kings God Raises Up</h3>

<p>The Bible&rsquo;s boldest claim about Persia is that the LORD deliberately raised up a pagan emperor &mdash; naming him &ldquo;my shepherd&rdquo; and &ldquo;his anointed&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/isa/44/28/" target="_blank" data-ref="Isaiah 44:28">Isaiah 44:28</a>; <a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/isa/45/1/" target="_blank" data-ref="Isaiah 45:1">45:1</a>) &mdash; to accomplish the restoration of His covenant people, &ldquo;though thou hast not known me&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/isa/45/5/" target="_blank" data-ref="Isaiah 45:5">Isaiah 45:5</a>; see <a href="/culture/ancient/achaemenid/05-cyrus-and-the-return/">Section 05</a>). Latter-day scripture teaches the same principle about the founding of the modern nation that would cradle the Restoration: &ldquo;I established the Constitution of this land, by the hands of wise men whom I raised up unto this very purpose&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/101?lang=eng&amp;id=p80#p80" target="_blank">D&amp;C 101:80</a>). The pattern is identical: God prepares the political conditions &mdash; a tolerant empire then, a nation of religious liberty now &mdash; so that His people can build, worship, and gather. Cyrus&rsquo;s decree of return is, in this sense, an Old Testament rehearsal of the way the Lord &ldquo;raises up&rdquo; rulers and frameworks to open the door for His work, working through the free choices and even the self-interest of those who do not know Him (see the governance-and-providence theme in <a href="/culture/ancient/achaemenid/03-how-the-empire-worked/">Section 03</a>).</p>

<div style="background: linear-gradient(135deg, #f5f3f0 0%, #ebe7e1 100%); padding: 20px 24px; border-radius: 8px; margin: 24px 0;">
<p style="margin: 0;"><strong>Two instruments, one Lord:</strong> set Persia beside Babylon and the lesson sharpens. Babylon&rsquo;s Nebuchadnezzar was called the Lord&rsquo;s &ldquo;servant&rdquo; to <em>judge</em> Judah (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/jer/25/9/" target="_blank" data-ref="Jeremiah 25:9">Jeremiah 25:9</a>; see the <a href="/culture/ancient/babylon/03-nebuchadnezzar-and-the-kings/">Babylon guide</a>); Persia&rsquo;s Cyrus was called the Lord&rsquo;s &ldquo;anointed&rdquo; to <em>restore</em> her. The same God who used one empire to scatter used the next to gather &mdash; sovereign over both, bending the rise and fall of superpowers to the good of His covenant. That is precisely the doctrine of Daniel&rsquo;s dream: kingdoms come and go, but &ldquo;the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/dan/4/17/" target="_blank" data-ref="Daniel 4:17">Daniel 4:17</a>).</p>
</div>

<br>

<hr>

<br>

<h3>The Return as the Pattern of the Gathering</h3>

<p>The single richest Latter-day Saint connection is the gathering of Israel. The tenth Article of Faith declares, &ldquo;We believe in the literal gathering of Israel and in the restoration of the Ten Tribes&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/a-of-f/1?lang=eng&amp;id=p10#p10" target="_blank">Articles of Faith 1:10</a>) &mdash; and the return from Babylon under Persia is the Old Testament&rsquo;s clearest working example of exactly that: a scattered, exiled people brought home by the hand of God to rebuild their worship and their city. The Book of Mormon frames the whole sweep of covenant history as scattering-and-gathering &mdash; &ldquo;the Lord God has led away from time to time from the house of Israel, according to his will and pleasure&rdquo; and remembers &ldquo;all them who have been broken off&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/10?lang=eng&amp;id=p22#p22" target="_blank">2 Nephi 10:22</a>) &mdash; and promises that &ldquo;the Lord will set his hand again the second time to restore his people&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/25?lang=eng&amp;id=p17#p17" target="_blank">2 Nephi 25:17</a>). The return under Cyrus was the <em>first</em> time in miniature; the latter-day gathering is that same covenant faithfulness on a global scale.</p>

<p>The keys that direct this latter-day gathering were restored in the Kirtland Temple, when &ldquo;Moses appeared&hellip; and committed unto us the keys of the gathering of Israel from the four parts of the earth, and the leading of the ten tribes&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/110?lang=eng&amp;id=p11#p11" target="_blank">D&amp;C 110:11</a>). For Latter-day Saints, then, Ezra&rsquo;s caravans on the road from Babylon and Nehemiah&rsquo;s builders on the walls of Jerusalem are not merely ancient history &mdash; they are the ancestors, in type, of the missionary work and temple-gathering the Restoration understands itself to be carrying out.</p>

<br>

<hr>

<br>

<h3>Temple Restoration, Then and Now</h3>

<p>The heart of the return was a <strong>temple</strong>. Everything in Ezra bends toward rebuilding the house of the Lord (see <a href="/culture/ancient/achaemenid/06-rebuilding-jerusalem/">Section 06</a>) &mdash; and Haggai&rsquo;s promise over that modest Second Temple, &ldquo;the glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/hag/2/9/" target="_blank" data-ref="Haggai 2:9">Haggai 2:9</a>), speaks directly to a people whose own restoration began with the command to build temples and whose scripture calls this &ldquo;a marvelous work and a wonder&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/25?lang=eng&amp;id=p17#p17" target="_blank">2 Nephi 25:17</a>). The parallels a Latter-day Saint teacher can draw are natural and unforced: opposition and discouragement stalling the work, prophets (Haggai, Zechariah) stirring the people to finish, sacrifice and scarcity given to build God&rsquo;s house before one&rsquo;s own &ldquo;cieled&rdquo; houses (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/hag/1/4/" target="_blank" data-ref="Haggai 1:4">Haggai 1:4</a>), and the tears of those who remember former glory giving way to the promise of greater glory to come. The Second Temple restoration is a template for every gathered people who has ever had to rebuild the house of the Lord against odds.</p>

<br>

<hr>

<br>

<h3>Ezra and the Return to the Word</h3>

<p>The exile turned Israel into a people of the book (see the <a href="/culture/ancient/babylon/07-the-exile-experience/">Babylon guide</a>), and the return produced its exemplary teacher: <strong>Ezra</strong>, who &ldquo;prepared his heart to seek the law of the LORD, and to do it, and to teach&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/ezr/7/10/" target="_blank" data-ref="Ezra 7:10">Ezra 7:10</a>), reading the scriptures aloud to the whole assembled people and giving &ldquo;the sense&rdquo; so they could understand (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/neh/8/8/" target="_blank" data-ref="Nehemiah 8:8">Nehemiah 8:8</a>). That scene &mdash; an entire covenant community gathered to hear, understand, and covenant around the word of God &mdash; is deeply resonant for a church built on scripture study, on &ldquo;seek, do, teach&rdquo; as a pattern of discipleship, and on the conviction that a people are renewed when they return to the word. Ezra&rsquo;s covenant-renewal assembly is, in its way, an ancient general conference.</p>

<br>

<hr>

<br>

<h3>Esther: Providence and &ldquo;Such a Time as This&rdquo;</h3>

<p>The book of Esther offers a different but equally Latter-day lesson: how God works when He is not visibly working (see <a href="/culture/ancient/achaemenid/07-esthers-world/">Section 07</a>). Esther never names God, yet its every turn testifies to His hidden hand &mdash; and Mordecai&rsquo;s question to the hidden queen, &ldquo;who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/est/4/14/" target="_blank" data-ref="Esther 4:14">Esther 4:14</a>), has become one of the most-quoted summons to purpose in Latter-day Saint teaching. It speaks to the doctrine that God places His children in particular times, families, and circumstances for reasons that matter &mdash; that we are &ldquo;come to the kingdom&rdquo; on purpose, and are asked, like Esther, for the courage to act when our moment comes. And the deliverance the book records &mdash; the preservation of the covenant people from the first empire-wide attempt to destroy them &mdash; is one more instance of the promise that the Lord remembers &ldquo;them who have been broken off&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/10?lang=eng&amp;id=p22#p22" target="_blank">2 Nephi 10:22</a>) and will not let His covenant fail.</p>

<div style="background: linear-gradient(135deg, #f5f3f0 0%, #ebe7e1 100%); padding: 20px 24px; border-radius: 8px; margin: 24px 0;">
<p style="margin: 0;"><strong>Teaching this material:</strong> three through-lines carry the whole Persian period for a class. <strong>(1) God governs the nations</strong> &mdash; He raised up Cyrus exactly as He later raised up &ldquo;wise men&rdquo; for the Constitution, preparing the world for His work. <strong>(2) Scattering is never the end</strong> &mdash; the return from Babylon is the pattern of the gathering the Restoration is still carrying out, temple by temple and convert by convert. <strong>(3) Providence is often quiet</strong> &mdash; from Esther&rsquo;s unnamed God to Ezra&rsquo;s &ldquo;good hand of my God upon me&rdquo; (<a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/kjv/ezr/7/9/" target="_blank" data-ref="Ezra 7:9">Ezra 7:9</a>), this era teaches students to recognize the Lord&rsquo;s hand in circumstances that look, from inside, like mere history. The Old Testament does not end in the ashes of exile; it ends with a decree of return, a rebuilt temple, and a people gathered home &mdash; which is exactly the shape of the hope the Restoration proclaims.</p>
</div>
