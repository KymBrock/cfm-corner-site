## A Note on This Guide

If Babylon is the empire that carried Judah away, Persia is the empire that brought Judah back. The Achaemenid Empire — the first true world empire, founded by Cyrus the Great — is the stage on which the Old Testament reaches its resolution: the exile ends, the temple rises again, Jerusalem's walls are rebuilt, the law is restored, and a hidden Jewish queen saves her people in the palace of the king.

<br>

Understanding Persia is essential for reading the last act of the Hebrew Bible honestly — Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther, the post-exilic prophets Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi, and the Persian-court chapters of Daniel. It is the world of the decree of Cyrus and the Cyrus Cylinder, of satraps and the Royal Road, of irrevocable laws and multilingual couriers, of Ahura Mazda and the fire altars — and of a small restored province praying, by royal command, for the life of the king who let them go home.

<br>

We approach Persia's history, religion, and archaeology with respect for the evidence, and with an eye toward what that evidence reveals about the God who called Cyrus "my shepherd" and "his anointed" — the God who governs the rise and fall of empires and turns even the exile into a homecoming.

<br>
