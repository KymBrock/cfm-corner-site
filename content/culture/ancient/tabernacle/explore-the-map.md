  <summary><span class="intro-toggle__label">Introduction</span></summary>
  <div class="intro-toggle__content">
    <p>The Tabernacle was the <a class="term-ref" href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/search/search.cfm?Criteria=%D7%9E%D6%B4%D7%A9%D6%B0%D7%81%D7%9B%D6%B8%D6%BC%D7%9F&t=KJV" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" data-ref="mishkan" data-popup-text="Hebrew term meaning &quot;dwelling place&quot; or &quot;tabernacle.&quot; It emphasizes that the sanctuary was the place where God chose to dwell among His covenant people."><em>mishkan</em> (מִשְׁכָּן)</a> — the dwelling place God commanded Israel to build so that He could live among His people. Scripture uses three names for the same structure, each revealing a different dimension of its purpose: <a class="term-ref" href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/search/search.cfm?Criteria=%D7%9E%D6%B4%D7%A7%D6%B0%D7%93%D6%B8%D6%BC%D7%A9%D7%81&t=KJV" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" data-ref="mikdash" data-popup-text="Hebrew term meaning &quot;sanctuary&quot; or &quot;holy place,&quot; highlighting the Tabernacle as a space set apart to God."><em>mikdash</em> (מִקְדָּשׁ)</a>, a sanctuary set apart as holy; <a class="term-ref" href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/search/search.cfm?Criteria=%D7%9E%D6%B4%D7%A9%D6%B0%D7%81%D7%9B%D6%B8%D6%BC%D7%9F&t=KJV" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" data-ref="mishkan" data-popup-text="Hebrew term meaning &quot;dwelling place&quot; or &quot;tabernacle.&quot; It emphasizes that the sanctuary was the place where God chose to dwell among His covenant people."><em>mishkan</em> (מִשְׁכָּן)</a>, a dwelling where God takes up residence; and <a class="term-ref" href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/search/search.cfm?Criteria=%D7%90%D6%B9%D7%94%D6%B6%D7%9C%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D6%B9%D7%A2%D6%B5%D7%93&t=KJV" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" data-ref="ohel mo'ed" data-popup-text="Hebrew phrase meaning &quot;tent of meeting,&quot; the appointed place where God met with His people."><em>ohel mo'ed</em> (אֹהֶל מוֹעֵד)</a>, the tent of meeting where God encounters His covenant people. One space. Three truths: it is holy, it is inhabited, and it is relational.</p>
    <p>This field guide does not claim to fix every symbol into one definitive meaning. It presents the patterns, connections, and interpretive traditions that help the Tabernacle read as a sanctuary of covenant approach, sacred order, and divine dwelling.</p>
