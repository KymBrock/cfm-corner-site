The united kingdom that David built and Solomon adorned lasted exactly three reigns. At **Shechem**, where the tribes had gathered to crown Solomon's son Rehoboam, a single arrogant answer split the nation in two: the northern ten tribes broke away under **Jeroboam** to become *Israel*, while the south, loyal to David's line, became *Judah*. The rest of the Old Testament's history — the rival altars, the prophets, the long slide to two exiles — flows from that fracture.

This field guide walks the split and what the book of **Kings** makes of it: the tax revolt that broke the kingdom, the golden calves that defined the north's worship, and the distinctive lens — scholars call it the **Deuteronomists'** — through which the whole story is told and judged.
