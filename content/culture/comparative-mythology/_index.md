The nations that surrounded Israel told their own stories of how the world began, how a
great flood once swept it away, of a tree at the center of everything, and of gods who
ordered the chaos and hung lights in the sky. Read side by side, these stories are neither
identical to Scripture nor wholly foreign to it. They rhyme.

This guide gathers those echoes — from Mesopotamia, Egypt, Canaan, and far beyond — and reads
them the way the site's [Ugarit & Canaanite Religion](/culture/ancient/canaan/04-ugarit-and-canaanite-religion/)
page already reads Baal and Asherah: not as the sources the Bible borrowed from, but as the
scattered, half-remembered fragments of an older worship that Israel preserved and the nations
diluted. Comparative mythology, held this way, does not threaten the text. It shows how widely
its themes were once known.
