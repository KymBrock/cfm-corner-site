The Divine Name: I AM THAT I AM ▶︎

This week introduces one of the most significant theological moments in all of scripture: God's revelation of His name at the burning bush. When Moses asks, "What is his name?" God responds with the mysterious phrase אֶֽהְיֶ֖ה אֲשֶׁ֣ר אֶֽהְיֶ֑ה (Ehyeh asher Ehyeh) — often translated "I AM THAT I AM."

The Hebrew verb hayah (הָיָה) means "to be" or "to become." The form Ehyeh is first-person: "I will be" or "I am." This is not a static philosophical statement about existence — it is a promise of presence. God is saying, "I will be with you. I will be whatever you need me to be."

The sacred name YHWH (יהוה) — rendered "LORD" in most English Bibles — is the third-person form of the same verb: "He Is" or "He Will Be." This name appears over 6,800 times in the Hebrew Bible. Every time you see "LORD" in small capitals, you are reading this divine name.

The Covenant Connection ▶︎

In Exodus 6:2–8, God explicitly connects His name to the covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob:

"I am YHWH. And I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob... but by my name YHWH was I not known to them. And I have also established my covenant with them... And I have also heard the groaning of the children of Israel... and I have remembered my covenant."

The patriarchs knew God as El Shaddai (God Almighty), but the full significance of YHWH — the covenant-keeping God who acts in history to redeem His people — would be revealed through the Exodus itself.

Moses: The Reluctant Prophet ▶︎

Moses is perhaps the most human of all the prophets. At the burning bush, he offers five objections to his calling:

  1. "Who am I?" (3:11) — Inadequacy
  2. "What is his name?" (3:13) — Authority
  3. "They will not believe me" (4:1) — Credibility
  4. "I am not eloquent" (4:10) — Ability
  5. "Send someone else" (4:13) — Refusal

God answers each objection, sometimes with signs, sometimes with promises, and finally with righteous anger when Moses simply refuses. Yet God still works with Moses, providing Aaron as spokesman.

This pattern teaches us something important: God does not wait for perfect instruments. He works through reluctant, flawed people who finally say yes — even when that yes is halfhearted.

Themes to Watch ▶︎
  • Water and Salvation: Moses drawn from the water (his name means "drawn out"), foreshadowing Israel's deliverance through the Red Sea
  • The Burning Bush: Fire that does not consume — God's presence that sanctifies rather than destroys
  • Holy Ground: Remove your sandals, for the place where you stand is holy
  • Hardened Hearts: The tension between Pharaoh's choices and God's purposes
